The Portfolio Story This Period

Every Scaling Step Went In and Every Account Gained Auction Share

Ocean City Has Closed Most of the Gap to Berger Without the Conquest Campaign Even Built Yet

At the last call we sat at 14.04% of the Ocean City auction against Berger Realty at 28.59%, a gap of 14.55 points and more than double our share. The conquest campaign is still to be built, so the lever that was designed to close this gap has not yet been pulled.

The Summer Flight Is Reaching Saturation in All Three Markets, and the Fix Is Not More Budget

On a like for like daily basis the flight is now buying 24.1% less reach for the same money, with CPM up 30.4% across the portfolio. Critically, click-through rate went up, not down, in all three markets. That combination rules out creative fatigue and points squarely at a finite local audience being repeatedly served. This is the single most important strategic finding of the fortnight and it shapes the plan below: widen the geography into the feeder markets, tighten frequency and rotate creative, rather than pushing more spend into the same pool.

Why Rising CPM With Rising Click-Through Is Good News, Not Bad

If the creative were tiring we would see click-through fall as cost rose. We are seeing the opposite, so the ads are landing better than they did at launch. What is happening is that three small shore geographies contain a finite number of reachable households, and after four weeks of delivery we are serving the same people more often. That is exactly the point at which an audience build should widen rather than deepen. Three moves follow from it: extend geography into the Pennsylvania and North Jersey feeder markets where the families actually come from, tighten the frequency cap so budget buys new households rather than repeat impressions, and bring the second creative concept into rotation. All three widen the pool that next season's retargeting will draw against.
